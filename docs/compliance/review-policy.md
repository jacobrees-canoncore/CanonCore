# Review policy and complaint-handling timeframes

**INTERNAL RECORD — NOT PUBLISHED**, except that the complaint-handling timeframes are also stated in
the public [reporting and complaints procedure](../../content/legal/reporting-and-complaints.md), because
`s.21(3)` requires the terms to specify the policies and processes governing complaint handling.

Kept to satisfy `s.9(3)–(4)`, `s.23(6)`, `s.36(3)` and **ICU D12.4(b)**.

## Review cycle

| Record | Reviewed |
| --- | --- |
| [Illegal content risk assessment](illegal-content-risk-assessment.md) | At least every 12 months (`s.9(3)`), and **before** any significant change to the design or operation of the service (`s.9(4)`) |
| [Children's access assessment](childrens-access-assessment.md) | Not more than one year apart (`s.36(3)`); also before a relevant significant change, on evidence of reduced age assurance effectiveness, or on evidence of a significant increase in child users (`s.36(4)`) |
| [Code measures register](code-measures-register.md) | Alongside the risk assessment |

**Also triggered by Ofcom.** Where Ofcom makes a significant change to a Risk Profile relevant to this
service, the risk assessment is reviewed. The operator subscribes to Ofcom's online safety email updates
so that such changes are noticed. Ofcom's own pages link superseded versions, so guidance is always
resolved through the [regulatory documents index](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/online-safety-regulatory-documents).

**Changes that require the risk assessment to be redone before they ship** are listed at the end of the
risk assessment. In short: any user upload, any messaging or commenting, linkifying user free text,
search across other users' content, a recommender, or a marketplace.

Ofcom's proportionality point is noted: "a relatively minor change on a large service is likely to have a
significant impact, while it could take a much larger change on a smaller service to trigger the need to
review their risk assessment" (Risk Assessment Guidance, Table 14).

## Retained versions

Superseded versions are retained for **at least three years**, per the Record-Keeping and Review Guidance
§2.5–2.6. Records are dated when made and on each update, and are made contemporaneously. Version history
is carried by this repository.

## Complaint-handling timeframes

**ICU D12.4(b)** requires complaints to be handled "within timeframes the provider has determined are
appropriate". Ofcom sets no numeric standard for a smaller low-risk service — the measures that would
(C4, C5, D4) do not apply — so these are the provider's own determination and are recorded here to make
them auditable.

> **DRAFT — confirm these are ones you can actually meet.** A missed self-imposed target is worse
> evidence than a slower one that is kept. They are deliberately unambitious for that reason.

| Complaint type | Target | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Report of intimate image content (`s.20A`) | **Takedown within 48 hours** of receipt | Statutory (`s.10(3A)`). The only hard number in the regime, and it is a takedown deadline rather than a response one |
| Report of other illegal content | Reviewed within **2 working days** | ICU D7.2(b) "promptly"; C2 "swiftly" for the resulting takedown |
| Appeal against a takedown or an account restriction | Determined within **5 working days** | ICU D9.2 "promptly" |
| Complaint that the service is not complying with `s.10`, `s.20` or `s.22` | Responded to within **10 working days** | ICU D12.4(b), provider's own determination |

**No acknowledgement of receipt is promised**, and none is required: ICU D4, which would require
acknowledgement and indicative timeframes, applies only to large or medium/high-risk services. Sending one
is good practice and is done where practicable, but it is deliberately not a commitment.
